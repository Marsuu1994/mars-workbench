Daily rhythm

Exploration · awaiting owner review

Smart reminders and low-friction tracking for the planner: two scheduled rituals bracket the day (Open the day · Close the day), at most one state-driven nudge sits between them, every notification body is glanceable, and the app badge plus a re-entry strip catch whatever a notification missed. Reasoning, options and phasing live in design/spike/daily-rhythm-notifications.md.

01 · The rhythm — two rituals, one nudge, quiet otherwise

A day in notifications. The two rituals are anchored to routines the user already has; the follow-up only fires if the morning was missed; the midday nudge only fires when the board's own state (the risk engine, the morning plan) justifies it. The body carries the plan itself — a glance already did the job even when nothing gets tapped.

Why it's shaped like this

  • B = MAPA prompt converts only when the person can act on it right now. Rituals sit at day boundaries; anything that can't be answered in one tap doesn't ship as a notification.
  • GlancePeople see most notifications within minutes but act on few. Putting the tasks in the body makes seeing the value — the "busy, missed it" case still got the reminder.
  • BreakpointsA missed morning gets one follow-up at lunch, never a drip. Every ignored alert lowers the odds for the next one, so scarcity is the feature.
  • Event cueThe midday nudge is not a time — it's a state: a rollover at the risk engine's 15:00 danger line, a focus task's slot starting, a card stuck in In Progress since yesterday.
  • Peak-endThe evening message leads with what got done. When everything's done the notification itself is the reward and opens nothing.
  • Fresh startMondays and mornings ask for commitment; evenings and Sundays only reflect and offer.
  • iOSAction buttons exist on Android only; on iOS the tap opens the quick-update sheet (screen 03). Web Push needs the app on the home screen (it already is).
02 · Open the day — the Today sheet

Tapping the morning notification (or opening the app on a not-yet-planned day) lands here: everything that could be today, grouped. Tap to pick; Start the day pulls the picks onto the board in one batch (BACKLOG → TODO). A capacity hint from the user's own history keeps the plan finishable; one focus star with an optional slot turns the plan into an if-then intention. Rows are tappable in this mockup.

What the sheet is doing

  • Plan → quietMaking a concrete plan removes the nagging feeling of open tasks (Zeigarnik; Masicampo & Baumeister). The morning pick is that plan — three taps, not a form.
  • If-thenThe focus star plus an optional slot ("afternoon") forms an implementation intention — the single most robust follow-through effect in the literature — and arms the midday nudge with a real moment.
  • Capacity"Your usual Tuesday ~6 pts" is computed from the user's own doneAt history. Going over is a soft, warm hint, never a block — it fights the planning fallacy so the evening card stays positive.
  • Model fitGroups map 1:1 onto existing states: rollovers are already on the board, dailies/weeklies come from the backlog, Do First items come from the matrix via Track This Week.
  • Re-entryThe sheet auto-opens once when the app is launched on an unplanned day — opening the app for any reason completes the loop a missed push started. "Skip today" is a real choice and ends the day's asks.
  • OpenCap: "up to 3 focus picks", a points cap from the hint, or hint only? (spike, open question 1)

Triggers (all from state the app already has)

  • SlotThe focus task's slot begins → "Afternoon: Write report is up." [Start] [Later]
  • 15:00A rolled-over daily is still open at the engine's danger line → "expires tonight. Done, or let it go?" [Done] [Let it go]
  • StaleA card in In Progress since yesterday → "done, or still going?" [Done] [Still going]
  • BudgetAt most one per day; the most severe wins; suppressed if the app was opened in the last two hours (they've seen the board). "Later" / "Not now" never re-fires the same day.
  • Two answersEach nudge is a binary question about one task. That is what makes it answerable while busy — and what makes "update from the notification" honest instead of a mini-app in a banner.
  • PlatformNotification buttons: Android only. The quick sheet is the canonical path; buttons are the Android bonus. Both hit the same batch status action.
04 · Close the day — recognition, not recall

The evening ritual is where tracking actually happens. Every open board task gets one of three answers — Done · Still on it · Not today — and Close the day commits them as one batch. Nothing has to be remembered mid-work; it only has to be recognised at night. Segments are tappable in this mockup.

Why the tracking moves here

  • RecognitionMoving a card while working is recall at the worst moment. Ticking a list at 21:00 is recognition — the cheapest memory operation there is (Nielsen). Batch reconciliation becomes the primary path; drag becomes a bonus.
  • Explicit fate"Not today" is a decision, not a failure. Giving every open task a fate is what lets the mind drop it for the night (the plan-making effect again).
  • Honest dataMarking a rollover done offers "did this yesterday" so doneAt credits the right day and the daily-average stat stays truthful.
  • One commitAll answers go in one batch action on tap of Close the day — no per-row round-trips, no half-closed days.
  • No shameThe header counts what's open, never what's "overdue". The day card that follows leads with what got done at any completion level.
  • OpenShould "Not today" on a daily task expire it now, or stay a no-op that lets rollover run? (spike, open question 4)
05 · The day card — feedback that pays for the ritual

Right after the commit: what got done (leading), a comparison against the user's own history, the process streak (rewards closing the day, not the outcome — and forgives a miss), where the week stands, and tomorrow's preview so the next morning is cheaper. Ends with an offer, never an ask.

Feedback rules

  • ProgressSmall visible wins are the strongest everyday motivator (Amabile & Kramer). Every status change moves the ring immediately; the card is where the day's movement is seen at once.
  • Own baselineComparisons are against the user's own history ("your Tuesday usual"), never an abstract target. At low completion the copy still leads with what got done: "2 of 4 — that's a day. Tomorrow has 2 dailies waiting."
  • InformationalNumbers, not judgement (self-determination theory: informational feedback sustains intrinsic motivation; controlling feedback erodes it). No confetti on every tap, no levels.
  • Process streakThe streak counts days closed, not tasks done — it rewards the habit we actually need. One shield a week avoids the streak-break cliff (the reason Duolingo sells freezes). Alternative: "11 of the last 14 days" — nothing to break.
  • Goal gradientThe week bar shows how close, not how much is left; effort accelerates near a goal.
  • TomorrowThe preview is a pre-commitment that makes the next morning cheaper — and the Dump link is the release valve for whatever is still looping.
06 · Cheaper updates + the settings that keep it trusted

Two supporting pieces. Board: a pending-ritual strip is the in-app re-entry surface for a missed push, and every mobile card gets a visible one-tap advance control (a drag alternative, always visible — never hover-only). Settings: a "Daily rhythm" card owns the two times, the nudge toggle and the permission ask, which happens here in context, once.

Settings · the Daily rhythm card

Supporting rules

  • Re-entryThe strip shows whichever ritual is pending and the sheet auto-opens once per ritual per day. A missed push is harmless because the app itself carries the reminder the next time it's opened for any reason.
  • BadgeThe home-screen icon badge counts open board tasks (or 1 while a ritual is pending): ambient, silent, survives a dismissed alert. Works on Android and on iOS 16.4+ home-screen web apps.
  • AdvanceOne tap replaces a drag on mobile; it is always visible (the hidden-pencil lesson). Forward-only, like the board. Desktop keeps drag and gains the same control.
  • ControlTwo time pickers, one toggle, weekends inherit plan mode. No sound settings, no frequency sliders — the budget is the product's promise, not a preference.
  • PermissionThe browser prompt is asked from this card only, after the value is shown. A denied prompt is nearly unrecoverable, so it is never asked on first launch.
  • Phase 0Everything on this screen plus screens 02 · 04 · 05 works with no push at all — it is the foundation, and already solves the tracking pain.
