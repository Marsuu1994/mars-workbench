Three kinds of work, one week on the board

Work splits into habits (fixed routines, counted per week), projects (ordered steps that never expire) and one-offs (the priority matrix, now with due dates). The week is planned once; Todo is today, Done is the week, and Doing is gone. Cards say which kind they are first, and risk is redrawn per kind. Fixture: Wednesday Oct 7, 10:00 — week 41.

Rules

1Risk, per kind

Each kind gets the one risk that is real for it. Red is reserved for a real deadline — only one-offs have one. The other two top out at amber, and their amber means "pace" or "size", never "failed".

KindSignalAmber whenRed when
HabitWeek dotsWhat's left needs every remaining day (left ≥ days left)Never
StepTimes carriedCarried 2× or more — split it?Never
One-offDue dateDue within 2 daysDue today or overdue

No clock thresholds: Todo already means today, and leftovers get an answer in Close the day. The risk code shrinks to three small rules.

2Habits expire quietly

A missed habit day doesn't roll over and never turns red. It becomes a faint dot in the habit's week and the count simply doesn't move. Weekly habits (3× / week) keep their unused instances until Sunday, then reset with the week.

3One-offs get an optional due date

Set when adding or editing a priority task, shown on the matrix card and on the board. No due date = no risk at all — "old" no longer counts as "urgent". The quadrant keeps saying how important it is; the date says when.

4No Doing column

Most cards are one sitting (1–3 h), so "in progress" lasted hours and told the board nothing. Multi-day work stays in Todo: Close the day's Tomorrow keeps it there, Not today sends it back to the backlog. The daily-rhythm focus star marks the one you're on.
